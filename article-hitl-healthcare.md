# Human-in-the-Loop for Healthcare

Grade clinical actions by patient consequences, not by whether they look administrative. A wrong note, routing decision or scheduling instruction can affect care as well as an explicit treatment recommendation.

## Grade the actions

These are starting points. Data sensitivity, reversibility and cumulative impact can raise the grade; the [framework](framework.html#2-grade-the-action) defines the rule.

| Action | Starting grade | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Draft a note from authorized records | Depends on clinical use | Keep the draft separate from the signed record. |
| Send routine administrative information | G1–G2 | Verify patient and destination. |
| Recommend a consequential clinical action | G2–G3 | Require qualified review with patient context. |
| Execute a high-stakes treatment change | G3 | Use an explicitly governed clinical workflow. |

## Set the boundaries

Define intended use, patient population, prohibited actions and escalation routes. Enforce record access and role permissions. Validate the complete workflow, including missing data and delayed human response.

## Make review useful

Show the source record, uncertainty, proposed action and alternatives. Give the clinician time and authority to change the decision. Route urgent cases to a staffed path; a review queue is unsafe if nobody can respond in time.

## Test the workflow

Measure missed hazards, inappropriate interventions, subgroup differences and time to escalation. Test incorrect patient identity, outdated records and unavailable reviewers.

A [September 2026 JAMA Health Forum commentary](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2854664) discusses oversight of agentic medicine. It is a policy perspective, not clinical validation. This framework does not establish clinical safety or satisfy all local regulatory requirements.
