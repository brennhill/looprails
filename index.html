The core idea

Most oversight breaks down in one corner.

How bad could an error be? Can someone detect and stop it in time? Use both answers to choose review, containment or bounded autonomy.

Consequence → Controllability →

Light touchLow stakes · easy to catch and undo
Danger zone

Review needs stronger controls

When a reviewer cannot catch an error in time, add stronger controls: stage execution, limit effects, restrict permissions or transfer the decision.

The method

Four steps: Grade · Guard · Show · Prove.

Apply the method to each action and to the combined effects of the run.

STEP 1

Grade

Rate reversibility, reach and stakes. Use G0–G3 as a starting design heuristic.

STEP 2

Guard

Enforce permissions, containment, limits and recovery before actions execute.

STEP 3

Show

Show the exact action, evidence and recovery limits. Give reviewers time and authority to decide.

STEP 4

Prove

Seed realistic mistakes. Measure missed hazards, useful interventions and false blocks.

FAQ

Common questions

What is human-in-the-loop (HITL) in AI?

A person contributes to, reviews or can change an AI system’s decisions. For agents, review needs useful evidence and real authority. Read the guide →

Does human-in-the-loop actually improve AI safety?

It can, when evidence, timing and authority support a useful decision. A September 2026 computer-use study found important limits in the interfaces it tested. See the evidence →

When should an AI agent require human approval?

When a consequential action needs authority or a reviewer can meaningfully check its effect. Existing authorization can cover bounded routine work. Read the approval guide →

What's the difference between human-in-the-loop and human-on-the-loop?

In-the-loop review happens before execution. On-the-loop supervision monitors running work and intervenes when needed. Both depend on time to act. Compare the modes →

How do you design human oversight for autonomous AI agents?

Grade the effects, enforce controls, show useful evidence and test the workflow. RAIL covers recovery, authorization, stopping and records. Read the framework →

What is the "lethal trifecta" for AI agents?

Private data, untrusted content and an outbound channel can enable disclosure. Restrict data and channels; read-only access can still leak information. Read the threat model →
